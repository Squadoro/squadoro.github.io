Sell or submit a card? Floor prices and the market tax

How to compare what a card brings on the market, after tax, with what it brings in an SBC.

Updated 11 November 2026 · 2 min read

Every tradeable card raises the same question: is it worth more sold or submitted? The answer depends on two numbers: its real sale price and what it replaces in an SBC.

The real sale price

On the transfer market, a 5% tax is taken from every sale. A card sold for 1,000 coins therefore brings you 950. For base cards, the price depends mostly on the rating: this is the floor price, the price of the cheapest cards at a given rating.

What the card replaces

In an SBC, a tradeable card replaces a card you would have had to buy, or another card from your club. If you have enough duplicates and untradeables to do the SBC, submitting an expensive tradeable card means losing its sale price.

A simple rule

  • Expensive tradeable card, not in your team and not essential for an SBC: sell it.
  • Cheap tradeable card: it's often worth more in an SBC than on the market, after tax.
  • Untradeable card: it can't be sold, so keep it for your team or submit it.

Tip Prices move a lot on the day of a big SBC: cards at the required rating go up. Selling that day and buying a few days later is often the right strategy.

Worked example

A tradeable 84-rated card sells for 1,600 coins. After the 5% tax, it brings you 1,520 coins. If it's used in a score-based SBC where an untradeable duplicate of the same rating could replace it, submitting it means losing those 1,520 coins. If, on the other hand, no other card can replace it, you'd have to buy an equivalent card at the same price: submitting it then costs nothing extra.

For a 300-coin card, selling only brings in 285 coins and takes time. In an SBC that needs it, it's often worth more.

Frequently asked questions

Where do Squadoro's prices come from? Floor prices by rating, collected every evening. You can adjust them in the settings, or enter a card's exact price on its card sheet.

What about tradeable special cards? Their price varies a lot from card to card: enter their price on their card sheet so the calculations take it into account.

In Squadoro

Squadoro estimates each card's value from floor prices collected every evening. The “Sell” group in “What should I do with my cards?” lists the tradeable cards that are worth more sold, with the total before and after tax.
