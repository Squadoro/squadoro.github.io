SBC fodder: which cards to submit first

Duplicates, untradeables, cheap cards: the order to use your cards in so you complete as many SBCs as possible without getting poorer.

Updated 11 November 2026 · 2 min read

Fodder is the name for the cards you hand in to SBCs. Choosing your fodder well is the difference between a club that gets richer with every event and a club that slowly empties.

The recommended order

  1. Untradeable duplicates. You can neither sell them nor keep them in your club: SBCs are their only use.
  2. Untradeables you don't play. They are worth nothing on the market; their only value is in SBCs.
  3. Cheap tradeables. A card that sells for a few hundred coins is often worth more in an SBC than on the market once the tax is taken off.
  4. Expensive tradeables, last, and only if the SBC is worth it.

What you should never submit

  • Players in your active squad and the ones who often play.
  • Loan players: they can't be used in SBCs.
  • Cards you're keeping for an announced SBC or a set you want to finish.

Keep some spare

The best SBCs often drop without warning. Keeping a small stock of Gold cards at different ratings (for example a few 82s, 83s and 84s) lets you do them straight away, without buying when prices go up.

Tip A card can only be used once. Before you submit an SBC, check that it doesn't take the only card a better SBC would have needed.

Example: sorting a pack opening

After opening a few packs, you get twelve Gold players. Three are untradeable duplicates: they go straight into the next score-based SBC. Two are tradeable and sell well: list them if you don't need them. The other seven, untradeable and mid-rated, become your fodder stock for classic SBCs, where their league and nation can help with chemistry.

Cards that count twice

Some low-value cards are precious in classic SBCs because they unlock a requirement: a rare player when the SBC asks for one, a player from an uncommon nation, or a player from a specific league. Before throwing them into a score-based SBC, check that a classic SBC won't need them.

Frequently asked questions

Should I keep all my high-rated Gold cards? Not all of them, but a small stock at each rating lets you do the good SBCs as soon as they come out, without buying when prices rise.

What should I do with Bronze and Silver cards? They're used in Bronze and Silver Upgrades and in SBCs that ask for them. On the market, they're worth very little.

In Squadoro

In “My club”, the “What should I do with my cards?” button sorts your cards into three groups: keep, use first in SBCs, and sell, with the reason for each card. Cards that the last calculation plans to use in your SBCs are at the top of the group to submit.
