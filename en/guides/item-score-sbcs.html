Score-based SBCs: understanding item score and avoiding overshoot

How score-based SBCs add up card points, why extra points are wasted, and how to finish as close as possible to the required total.

Updated 11 November 2026 · 3 min read

In a score-based SBC, each card is worth points depending on its rating: that's its item score. The SBC sets a total, for example 5,500 points, and sometimes a minimum or maximum rating. As soon as your selection reaches the total, the SBC can be submitted.

Points don't go up evenly

A card's score rises very quickly with its rating: an 85-rated card is worth far more than two 80-rated cards. That's what makes these SBCs useful for getting rid of mid-rated cards, but also tricky: a single card that's too strong can blow past the total.

Overshoot, an invisible loss

If the SBC asks for 5,500 points and you hand in 7,000, the 1,500 extra points don't carry over: they are lost. It's like paying 70 coins for something that costs 55. On an SBC you repeat several times, these losses add up.

  • Start with the cards that have no other use: duplicates and mid-rated untradeables.
  • Finish with a small card to get close to the total, rather than a big one.
  • If your club has no small card that fits, it is sometimes better to wait until you have one, or to buy a cheap one.

Minimum and maximum rating

A maximum rating (for example “max 83”) protects your best cards but forces you to stack more mid-rated ones. A minimum rating rules out Bronze and Silver cards. Both completely change which cards you should submit: check them before you choose.

Tip The required score isn't the only cost: look at how many cards it takes, too. Twenty small cards spent on one SBC may be missing for the next ones.

Worked example

Say an SBC needs 5,500 points, and depending on their rating your cards are worth a few hundred points for an 82 or 83, around 800 for an 84 and over 2,000 for an 85. With two 85s and an 84, you go far over the total. With one 85, two 84s and a few 83s, you can land within a few dozen points of it.

The second solution uses more cards, but cheaper ones: it is almost always the better choice. The exact values depend on the game; Squadoro uses a table by rating that you can adjust in the settings if the game shows different scores.

Frequently asked questions

Are special cards worth more points? They can be worth more than base cards of the same rating. Check the score shown in the game; in Squadoro, you can enter a card's exact score on its card sheet.

Can I submit an SBC in several goes? Some score-based SBCs let you add cards bit by bit. In Squadoro, enter the points already submitted: the solver only looks for what's missing.

Do loan cards count? No, loan cards can't be used in SBCs.
