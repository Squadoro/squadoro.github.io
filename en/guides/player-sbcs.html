Player SBCs: should you do it?

The questions to ask before giving away tens of thousands of coins for an untradeable special card.

Updated 11 November 2026 · 2 min read

Player SBCs give one specific special card, often tied to an event. They're among the most expensive SBCs in the game, and the card you receive can't be sold. Before you start, four questions are enough.

1. Does the player make your team?

That's the only really solid reason. If the player replaces a starter and improves your rating or chemistry, he's useful in every match. If not, he'll probably end up… in another SBC.

2. How much does it cost with your club?

The price shown by specialist websites assumes you buy everything on the market. With your duplicates and untradeables, the real cost can be much lower. That's the cost that matters.

3. How much time is left?

A player SBC often lasts a few days. Prices of the required cards usually go up on release day, then come back down: if you have to buy, waiting a little can save a lot, as long as you don't miss the end date.

4. What won't you be able to do anymore?

The fodder you use here won't be available for other SBCs. If the player SBC empties your stock of high-rated Gold cards, you may miss out on Upgrades or more profitable sets.

Tip In coins, a player SBC almost always loses value, since the card can't be resold. That's not a reason not to do it: it's a reason to do it for the right player.

Example: two player SBCs, two answers

First case: a defensive midfielder who would replace your starter, rated three points lower. The SBC is expensive, but you'll play this player every match for weeks: it's worth it if your club can pay for it mostly with duplicates.

Second case: a flashy striker, but you already have two better strikers. He'd end up on the bench, then in an SBC. Even if he's “cheap” for a player SBC, it's a net loss.

Frequently asked questions

Do player SBCs come back? Some return during events, but don't count on it.

Should I buy the fodder straight away? If the SBC lasts several days, prices of the required ratings often drop after the first day. If you have to buy, compare before rushing.

In Squadoro

Player SBCs get a large tile showing the rating of the card you'll receive. Squadoro works out their cost with your club and counts the card you receive at its value as fodder: that's why they're often near the bottom of the value ranking, even when they're worth it for your team.
