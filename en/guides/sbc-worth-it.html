How to tell if an SBC is worth it

Compare the value of the reward with the real cost of the cards you give away, taking untradeables and duplicates into account.

Updated 11 November 2026 · 3 min read

An SBC is worth it when what you get is worth more than what you give. That sounds obvious, but both sides of the scale are often misjudged: people overrate a random reward and forget that some of the cards they submit could have been sold.

The cost: what else you could have done with the cards

The real cost of a card isn't what you paid for it but what you lose by submitting it. A tradeable card is worth roughly its market sale price, minus the tax. An untradeable card can't be sold: it is only worth what it could bring in another SBC. An untradeable duplicate is worth even less, since you can't even keep it in your club.

That's why the same SBC can cost 500 coins for a player with lots of duplicates and 5,000 coins for someone who has to hand in tradeable cards.

The value: an average, not a hope

For a pack, think in terms of average value. A “2 Gold players 83+ pack” will usually give two players close to 83, and only rarely an expensive one. Since SBC rewards are untradeable, their real value is their value as fodder for other SBCs, unless a player you receive makes your team.

  • Good value: the average value of the reward is higher than the cost of your cards.
  • Break-even: the difference is small; worth doing if you need the reward.
  • Not worth it: you lose value; keep these for SBCs whose reward you really want, such as a player for your team.

Player SBCs

An SBC that gives a specific player is almost never profitable in coins: the card you receive can't be sold. It's worth it if the player genuinely improves your team, or if you can complete it with nothing but duplicates and untradeables that have no other use.

Tip A reward is only worth something if you use it. A Gold players pack you receive and never open brings you nothing.

Worked example

An SBC gives a pack of two Gold players 83+. Your club offers two ways to complete it. The first uses four tradeable cards that each sell for about 1,500 coins: a real cost of around 6,000 coins. The second uses six untradeable duplicates and one tradeable card worth 400 coins: a real cost of only a few hundred coins.

The reward, two untradeable players close to 83, has an average value roughly equal to what those cards bring as fodder in other SBCs. With the first solution, the SBC clearly loses value; with the second, it is worth it. The SBC is the same: your club is what changes the answer.

Estimation traps

  • Counting the best possible card in the pack instead of the average card.
  • Forgetting that the players you receive are untradeable: they can't be resold.
  • Forgetting the 5% tax when you compare with selling your own cards.
  • Ignoring the overshoot in a score-based SBC: the lost points are part of the cost.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the same SBC worth it for a friend but not for me? Because the cost depends on the cards each of you owns. Someone with plenty of duplicates of the right rating pays almost nothing.

Should I only do SBCs that are worth it? Not necessarily. An SBC that loses a little value can still be worth doing if it gives you a useful player or completes a set with a good final reward.
